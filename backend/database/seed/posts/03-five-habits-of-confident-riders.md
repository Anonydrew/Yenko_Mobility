After thousands of hours watching how people ride on campus, our safety team noticed something: the riders who never have close calls aren't necessarily the most skilled. They simply do a handful of small things, every single time.

Here are the five habits we'd teach every new rider.

## 1. Do the 30-second check

Before you ride away, give the bike a quick once-over:

- **Brakes:** squeeze both levers. They should feel firm, not spongy.
- **Tyres:** press down on the handlebars. The tyres shouldn't look flat.
- **Lights:** tap the light button on the display and check both lights come on.
- **Bell:** ring it once, so you know where it is before you need it.

If anything feels wrong, pick another bike and tap **Report an issue** in the app. Reports like this get the bike checked by our field team within hours.

## 2. Set the seat to your height

A seat that's too low ruins your balance and your knees. When you sit on the saddle, the balls of your feet should just touch the ground. The seat post on every Yenko E1 has height markings, so once you know your number you can set it in seconds.

## 3. Ride predictably

Other people can only avoid you if they can guess what you'll do next.

- Keep to the right, moving in the same direction as traffic.
- Signal before you turn by holding your arm out on the side you're turning towards.
- Don't weave between parked cars or cut across walkways.
- Make eye contact with drivers at junctions. If you can't see their eyes, they can't see you.

## 4. Slow down where people walk

The areas around lecture halls, libraries and halls of residence are full of people who are often looking at their phones. In these zones, the app automatically limits your speed to **12 km/h** and shades the map yellow. Ring your bell early and give pedestrians plenty of space. They always have right of way.

## 5. One rider, two hands, no distractions

Yenko bikes are built for one person. A passenger changes how the bike brakes and steers, and it's the most common cause of the falls we see. Keep both hands on the handlebars. If you need to check directions, pull over first, or set your route before you start if your phone is in the handlebar mount.

## And one more: wear a helmet

A helmet is the single best way to protect yourself if you do fall. Semester Pass holders can collect a free Yenko helmet from any campus hub. Just bring your student ID.

Ride safe, and see you on campus.
