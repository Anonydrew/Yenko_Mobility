When we launched Yenko, we rode every off-the-shelf e-bike we could get our hands on up the hill from the Legon main gate to the Balme Library. Most were designed for flat European cities. They overheated, their batteries faded by mid-afternoon, and their baskets couldn't hold a laptop bag without it bouncing out at the first speed bump.

So we designed our own. The **Yenko E1** is rolling out now, and by the end of October it will make up our entire fleet.

## What's new on the E1

- **Stronger pedal assist.** A 250 W rear hub motor tuned for climbs gives you smooth assistance up to 25 km/h. You still pedal, but hills stop feeling like a workout before your 8 am lecture.
- **A battery that doesn't run out on you.** The 36 V swappable battery gives up to 60 km per charge. Our field team swaps batteries before they drop below 25%, so the bike you pick is always ready.
- **Brakes you can trust in the rain.** Front and rear disc brakes stop confidently on wet roads during the rainy season.
- **Lights that switch on by themselves.** Integrated front and rear LEDs come on automatically at dusk and stay on for your whole ride.
- **A basket built for student life.** The front basket carries up to 8 kg and has a bungee strap, so your laptop bag, groceries or lab coat stay put.
- **Tyres that shrug off thorns.** Puncture-resistant tyres mean fewer bikes out of service and more bikes near you.

## Designed around how students actually ride

We interviewed more than 300 riders before finalising the design. Three requests came up again and again, and all three made it onto the E1.

**Quick seat adjustment.** The seat post has height markings, so you can set it to your number in seconds. Once you've found your height, save it in the app under *Profile → Ride preferences*.

**A phone holder that doesn't wobble.** The handlebar mount fits phones up to 6.9 inches and keeps navigation in view without you having to hold anything.

**Unlocking that works when the network doesn't.** The smart lock falls back to Bluetooth when mobile data is patchy, so you can start and end rides even in the basement of the library.

## Where to find it

The E1 is already live at the University of Ghana and KNUST, with the University of Cape Coast following next week. Open the Yenko app, tap the map and look for bikes with the lime-green **E1** badge.

Tell us what you think. Every piece of feedback sent through *Help → Share feedback* is read by our product team, and the next version of the E1 is already on the drawing board.
